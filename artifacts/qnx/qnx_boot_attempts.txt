QNX BOOT ATTEMPTS - none reached userspace
==========================================

Four attempts, two host architectures, two image types. All four load the QNX
kernel and then produce no further console output.

-------
  # | Host                     | Image                          | Last output
  --|--------------------------|--------------------------------|---------------------------
  1 | macOS arm64 (Apple M)    | mkqnximage disk (QNX-official) | "QNX v1.2d Boot Loader: .."
  2 | macOS arm64 (Apple M)    | mkqnximage disk, -serial file: | (no bytes captured)
  3 | macOS arm64 (Apple M)    | custom multiboot IFS           | "Booting from ROM.."
  4 | x86_64 EC2 (Ubuntu 22.04)| custom multiboot IFS           | "Booting from ROM.."
  5 | x86_64 EC2 (Ubuntu 22.04)| mkqnximage disk (QNX-official) | "QNX v1.2d Boot Loader: .."

  QEMU 9.x (macOS, Homebrew) and QEMU 6.2.0 (Ubuntu 22.04). Neither host had
  /dev/kvm: the Mac has no x86 hardware virtualisation at all, and the EC2
  instance was a t3.large, a virtualised guest where KVM is not exposed.

WHAT THIS RULES OUT
-------------------
Two earlier hypotheses are excluded by the evidence above:

  * NOT cross-ISA emulation. The first three attempts ran on Apple silicon,
    where qemu-system-x86_64 must translate x86 to ARM. Attempts 4 and 5 ran on
    a native x86_64 Linux host and failed identically, so ARM->x86 translation
    is not the cause.

  * NOT the hand-written IFS. Attempts 1, 2 and 5 used QNX's own unmodified
    mkqnximage output. It fails the same way as the custom image, so the fault
    is not in the IFS build file in this directory.

Console wiring was also checked rather than assumed: the IFS uses
"startup-x86 -D8250..115200" with devc-ser8250 on /dev/ser1, and SeaBIOS output
on that same serial path WAS captured in attempts 1, 3, 4 and 5. A silent serial
port is therefore not the explanation for the missing kernel output.


WHAT IS *NOT* ESTABLISHED
-------------------------
The absence of KVM is the strongest remaining hypothesis, and it is the reason
the next attempt should be made on a host with /dev/kvm. But it is a HYPOTHESIS,
not a demonstrated cause. Nothing here proves it, because:

  * No successful KVM boot was ever performed for comparison. Bare-metal EC2 was
    blocked by an account vCPU quota of 16 (x86 bare-metal types need 48-96);
    a quota increase was requested and was still pending.

  * No QNX documentation was consulted stating that QNX 8.0 requires hardware
    virtualisation, or that it is unsupported under QEMU TCG.

Alternative explanations that these attempts do NOT exclude:

  * Emulated-platform gaps. QNX's startup-x86 does low-level chipset, APIC and
    timer initialisation; TCG may model something it depends on differently or
    not at all. This would look identical to a KVM problem from the outside.

  * CPU feature mismatch. Both hosts used "-cpu qemu64". QNX may require
    instructions or MSRs that model does not provide. A different -cpu (e.g.
    host/max/Nehalem) was not tried.

  * Machine-type or firmware mismatch. Only "-M pc" with SeaBIOS was tried.
    UEFI was not, and mkqnximage exposes a --boot-mode=[default|uefi|qemu]
    option that was left at its default.

  * Insufficient time. TCG is slow. Attempts were bounded at 150-900s. A boot
    that is merely very slow rather than hung has not been ruled out, though
    900s with no single byte of kernel output makes this unlikely.

CONCLUSION
----------
"The portable core cross-compiles for QNX 8.0" is supported by the other files
in this directory. "SensorForge runs on QNX" is NOT supported by anything here
and is not claimed. The specific cause of the boot failure remains open; lack of
KVM is the leading suspect and the cheapest next thing to test, not a finding.
